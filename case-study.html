Recovery Equipment Checkout App

Multnomah Athletic Club · Portland, OR

The Recovery Equipment Checkout App was built for the Multnomah Athletic Club (MAC) in Portland, OR. It was designed to replace an existing system that was confusing and non-intuitive for both members and staff. The app streamlines the checkout experience with a cleaner interface while giving staff greater control, improving the experience on both sides of the counter. Built using Claude and deployed on Vercel as a working prototype, currently pending rollout at the club.

This app serves two distinct user groups with different needs.

Members

Members at the Multnomah Athletic Club range from teenagers to people in their 70s. Nearly all members who check out recovery equipment do it after their workout. Most are longtime members who have used the checkout system for years, with a small number of first-timers. For members, success looks like completing the check-out/in process in seconds without asking for help.

Staff

Staff members range from their early 20s to late 30s and interact with the app every single day. There are roughly 50+ checkouts per day from both longtime members and first-time users, with a third to half needing help with the app depending on the day. For staff, success looks like members checking equipment out with little to no troubleshooting, the app displaying accurate information, and being able to provide elevated service.

The original system left members confused and staff with no control. Members who had used it for years still needed assistance. What should have taken under 30 seconds often took twice that time or more.

Members

  • The UI was not intuitive. There was no clear indication that the member number field was at the top of the page.
  • There was no visual indication that member info was accepted. A small piece of text appeared underneath the text box, but members didn't read it or didn't notice it. Some would keep pressing "enter" over and over.
  • First-timers often didn't know to enter their info at the top, so they would stand there tapping on the equipment.
  • Text was small, so members with vision impairment needed assistance.
  • When members checked out equipment from the bottom of the page, the page didn't scroll back up to the text box, leading to confusion about where to enter member info.
  • Members could only check out one item at a time.

Staff

  • When equipment was out of commission, it still displayed as available, leading to confusion and frustrated members.
  • Usage timers didn't update. A manual refresh was needed to see the current time.
  • If a member forgot to check a piece of equipment back in, staff had to pull their info from a separate app, switch back to the system, and copy and paste it into the text box to check it back in.

The current system turned a simple task into a complex one. It was supposed to let members quickly check out the recovery equipment they needed while allowing staff to keep track of what was in use. But it was built as a single linear form, and if that linear pattern was broken at any point, the whole process broke with it. The fix was not just patching up the existing system, but rebuilding it from the ground up with consideration for both sides of the desk.

Split-screen layout

The original system had an unclear starting point at the top of the page, and first-timers would tap on equipment before entering their information. This led to confusion and needing staff to tell members to start at the top of the page. I rebuilt the user interface with the member info text box on the left and the equipment on the right. Rather than building on top of the current layout, the split provides a clear left-to-right format.

Why

In the original top-to-bottom layout, members often saw only the equipment and started there, not knowing they needed to enter their info first. A left-to-right split puts both steps in view at once, so the order is apparent without anyone having to explain it. It also follows the direction people naturally read.

Visual confirmation

When members entered their info and hit enter, there was no visual indication that it was accepted. Small text appeared underneath the text box, but no one noticed it. I added larger confirmation text and an arrow pointing toward the equipment. This provides a clear indication that the member's info was accepted and shows the next step in the process. The larger text also makes the confirmation readable for members who have difficulty reading the screen.

Why

I noticed members would enter their info and then freeze, unsure what the next step was. An arrow does more than confirm the entry was accepted, it points them toward the equipment. A checkmark or a color change would have signaled success without telling anyone where to go next.

Auto-scroll

In the older system, when a member checked out equipment at the bottom of the page, the view stayed put. The next person in line would start at the bottom with no indication to scroll up. I built two behaviors. The equipment page returns to the top after every checkout, and when a member enters their info to check something in, the page scrolls directly to the equipment they have out, then returns to the top for the next person. This resets the page to the same starting point for everyone.

Why

The reset gives everyone the same starting point. If the page is left somewhere in the middle, a member only sees part of the equipment and can't tell what's actually available. Auto-scrolling to a member's checked-out equipment on return saves them from hunting for it themselves.

Old
New

Out of commission mode

When equipment was broken or unavailable, the old system still showed it as available. Members would try to check it out and get frustrated when told it was unavailable, and staff had no way to mark it as out of commission. If staff wanted to mark something out of commission, they would have to contact the tech department to mark it out. I added a staff mode, accessed by typing "ooc" into the member info text box, that lets staff select which equipment to grey out. Now the equipment status can display available, checked out, or out of commission. This allows the staff to display the correct information for members, and it leads to less frustration and easier customer service.

Why

A typed command keeps the UI clean and avoids putting a button on screen that a member might press out of curiosity. I also chose not to put it behind a password, since passwords get forgotten and it's one more thing for staff to remember. The tradeoff is that the command isn't discoverable on its own, so new staff have to be shown it exists.

Old
New

Multi-item checkout

The original system only allowed one item per member at a time. Members often need more than one piece of equipment, like Normatecs and a Hyperice massage gun, but only one could be tracked. It also created a gap with guests, who don't have member numbers of their own. I made it so a single member's info can check out multiple items at once.

Why

Beyond the obvious case of members needing more than one item, this closes a tracking gap. A member bringing a guest can now check out equipment for both, so the club has an accurate record of what's in use rather than equipment leaving the floor unaccounted for.

Single-app check-in

When a member forgot to check their equipment back in, staff had to open a separate app, find the member's info, and enter it back into the system to close it out. The new system only needs the member's name, so staff can enter the name already shown in the box and check the item back in.

Why

This lets staff change equipment status without relying on a second app. A two-app workflow slows things down, and keeping everything in one place makes it more efficient.

The app has not been rolled out yet. I kept it web-based to match the existing system, which makes the transition easier for both members and staff. There is no new hardware and nothing to install.

Rolling it out would require approval from multiple departments, including fitness, technology, and the board. Staff onboarding should be straightforward since the flow is similar to the old system, with the main addition being the out-of-commission feature. For members, staff would give a heads-up during the first week that the system has changed.

Adapting to User Feedback

After working the desk for a while, I gathered feedback from members and coworkers about the system. At first I observed and listened, but as I noticed more frustration and confusion I started asking members and staff directly. The recurring theme, and my own main frustration, was the lack of visual confirmation.

Staff were frustrated that members couldn't check out multiple items at once. When a member brought a guest, we could only track the main member's items while the guest's went untracked. I added multi-item checkout so a single member's info can cover more than one piece of equipment.

What I Would Validate After Launch

Since the app hasn't rolled out yet, the improvements are still assumptions. The clearest test is the assistance rate. Right now a third to half of members need help on a given day, and if the new layout works that number should drop. I would also watch whether members still freeze after entering their info, which would tell me whether the arrow is doing its job, and whether checkout time comes closer to the under-30-seconds it should take.
